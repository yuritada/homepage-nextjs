# progateBar Report: Why Connections Between Engineers Matter

I made it out to progateBar again this month.

## What is progateBar?

It's an engineer meetup hosted by [progate](https://prog-8.com/). It draws people from all sorts of backgrounds — working engineers, students, people in the middle of a job search.

## Talking with the people there

This time I ended up talking mostly with backend engineers. A few things that stuck with me.

### "Communication matters more than code"

That's what an engineer five years into their career told me. Technical skill is a given, but once you're working on a team, what really separates people is the ability to build shared understanding and put things into words.

I lean hard toward the technical side, so that one hit home.

### There's more than one shape a career can take

- Join a startup as a full-stack engineer
- Go to a big company and learn solid design practices
- Go freelance and get your hands on all kinds of products

None of these is the one right answer — it depends on what you want to get out of it. Obvious enough when you say it out loud, but hearing it from people who've actually lived it brings the picture into much sharper focus.

## What I took away

**Putting your work out there matters**

"You can tell pretty much everything from someone's GitHub" is something I hear a lot. It was a fresh reminder of how much it matters to build things up in a form people outside can actually see — code, articles, a portfolio.

**Show up to communities**

Just turning up to something like this once a month brings in firsthand information about industry trends and what stacks different companies are running. Making a habit of going after information rather than waiting for it seems important.

## Wrapping up

Sharpening your skills and connecting with people both matter. I'd like to keep them in balance instead of leaning too far in either direction.

I'm planning to go again next month, so I'll write that one up too.
