Slides from a campus lightning talk aimed at the students a year below me. The original title was "How to Win a Hackathon." *Written in Japanese.*

The talk reframes a hackathon around the basic question of development — *which problem, solved how* — and breaks what judges weigh into three axes: finding and solving the problem, implementation, and the slides and demo. On planning specifically, it argues that pain ("this is tedious, this hurts") lands harder than gain ("this is fun"), and that picking a technology first and retrofitting an idea onto it is a trap.

It ends on the honest note that a hackathon is still a gamble. There is no guaranteed way to win — the real skill is being able to adjust to the situation you are handed.
